# Japanese Record Stores

Since I was a kid, records have held more value to me than other music
mediums. You have the larger artwork, some albums came with posters,
stickers or other things. The sleeves made convenienct spaces to save flyers,
ticket stubs and other band-related papers.

My collection consists mainly of late-70s / early-80s punk, but I have a wide
range of tastes. Whenever I travel, I try to visit one or two record stores.
Japan would end up a place that I truly wanted to shop for vinyl. Some
Japanese releases differ from albums released in other markets. For example,
some Japanese records from foreign artists would include the single b-side
tracks, as 45s did not take off well in Japan. And, some would have different
artwork, especially where conservative distributors would object to more
provocative album covers.

## Union Record

The first record store was in the busy Shibuya area of Tokyo, famous for the
[Shibuya Crossing][sc]. Considered the world's busiest pedestrian crossing,
up to 3,000 people cross the road with every light cycle. A few streets over,
I navigated my way to [Union Record][ur].

## Super Milk

In Kyoto, my good friend Gail pointed out [Super Milk][sm], a record store
tucked away in a shopping zone not far from our hotel. While not known for
the punk music I wanted to find, I did pick up a Japanese release of [Public
Image Limited's][wwwpil] unoffical live album, *Profile*. Recorded at Los
Angeles' Olympic Auditorium, in May 1980, many regard this double LP as their
best live performance.

![Profile][pil]

## Revenge Records

On the third floor of an Osaka office building sits [Revenge Records][rr].
When I came in, the sole customer, some grindcore metal played. I surveyed
the store, then focused on the six bins of punk records. By the time I
started flipping through the second bin, the proprietor switched things up,
playing some early punk (some compilation, but I don't know which bands).
I pulled out a few, then asked the shopkeep for similar Japanese bands. I
think things got lost in translation, as he just provided commentary on a few
foreign punk albums, then went back behind the counter to change the music.

**Revenge Records**

![Revenge Records][revenge]

**My Haul**

Funny to go all the way from Reno to Osaka, to purchase a record from a Reno
punk band. Finding the *Praise* EP by [7 Seconds][s7] made me happy, as I
have tried to collect all the band's releases and did not have this one.

A fan of [MDC][wwwmdc], finding a copy of their rarest live album, recorded
in 1990 in the then-Yugoslavia, ended up a treasure. The band okayed the
release, but did nothing to help distribute it outside that area of Europe.
Many people, even hardcore fans, wrongly consider this album a bootleg.

Other finds include the Belgian *Bloodstains Across...* compilation and the
first release from the California punk band [Neighborhood Brats][nb], plus
a few more.

![Records From Revenge Records][revengehaul]

## Suicidal

The tunes from Californian punk-turned-metal band, [Suicidal Tendencies'][st]
first album still gets heavy rotation in my playlists. Tight musicianship
and provocative lyrics, with songs *I Shot The Devil*, about assassinating
then-president Ronald Reagan and *I Saw Your Mommy*, a reveal that one person
knows the other paid to have his mother killed, caught my attention. The
band's more thought-provoking songs, like *Memories Of Tomorrow*, about the
state of things after nuclear war, and *Institutionalized*, talking about the
struggle of teens whose parents think are in need of therapy.

I do not listen to much of their music after they crossed over into metal,
but 1990's *Lights...Camera...Revolution!* contains the track
*You Can't Bring Me Down*, a rant against several things: Los Angeles had
banned Suicidal Tendencies from playing live in the county, citing the
possibility of violence. This included the debunked assertion that the band
belonged to a gang. The [Parents Music Resource Center][pmrc] had added the
band to their ban list. The song contains the following lyric:

> You wouldn't know what crazy was if Charles Manson was eating fruit loops
> on your front porch.
