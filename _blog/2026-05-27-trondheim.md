Colorful storehouses line the Nidelva, the river that cuts through Trondheim.
Once the capital of Norway, Trondheim remains quite the city, with its
university and tourism. This place has deep roots in history, as the site
where King Olav II ruled.

After declaring himself the king in 1015, Olaf Haraldsson (King Olav II)
tried to unite Norway. He ordered the killing of the five contending rulers
and promoted a single religion to replace the worship of Norse gods. He
funded writers to craft stories of his battles with trolls and giants. Stories
grew to include him controlling the weather for better fishing and healing the
sick. Within one year of his death, local bishops backed claims of Olaf's
beatification, though he did not officially become a saint until 1888.

![][a]

Built over the burial site of King Olav II, Nidaros Cathedral remains a
popular pilgrimage destination. While this church stood as the Roman Catholic
cathedral for the area since its construction in the late eleventh century, it
transitioned to the Evangelical Lutheran Church of Norway in the sixteenth
century. Either way, it remains the world's northernmost cathedral.

![][c]

Maritime Robotics designs autonomous drones for seafloor mapping, marine
research and military purposes. Headquarted in Trondheim, they bring in
engineering talent from around the globe, in addition to hiring from the
local university.
