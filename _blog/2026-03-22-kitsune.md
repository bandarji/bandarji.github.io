# Kitsune

The fox, or kitsune, possesses supernatural abilities in Japanese folklore.
Messengers and guardians for Inari, the Shinto deity of agriculture, the
kitsune statues appear outside Inari temples. They often wear red scarves or
bibs. These garments ward off evil. They also often carry items in their
mouths, such as a key for the granary, a spiritual power jewel, a wisdom
scroll or a sheaf of rice.

Here's two Kitsune wearing red bibs. One holds a key, the other a ball. The
ball is a magical orb called *hoshi no tama*, or *star ball*. The symbol
gives blessings for a good harvest. The kitsune stores its life force within
the ball and stories say that a person who steals the ball can force the
kitsune to grant them one wish. Typically, the person wishes for wealth, but
kitsune tricks them, as the coins turn into pebbles after the ball is
returned. If not returned, the kitsune dies and the thief lives out their
days with terrible luck.
