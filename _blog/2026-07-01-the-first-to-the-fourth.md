# Fourth of July

On our way from Halifax to Miami, we stopped in Boston.

A few crazy Boston facts:

- First lit on September 14, 1716, [Boston Light][bl] is the first lighthouse
built in America. It was destroyed at some point during the American
Revolution, but reconstructed in 1783.
- The [Boston University Bridge][bub] is a place where a boat can pass under a
train, passing under a car, passing under an airplane. Come claim this is the
only place in the world, however Portland's [Steel Bridge][sb] has a similar
configuration.
- A storage tank burst in 1919, unleashing more than two million gallons of
boiling molasses. The [Great Molasses Flood][gmf] killed 21 and injured 150.
The Dead Milkmen song [*The Great Boston Molasses Flood*][dms] uses the event
to poke fun at regulations and corporate negligence.
- Puritans viewed Christmas as a pagan custom and made it
[illegal to celebrate][noxmas] from 1659 until 1681. Anyone caught observing
risked a five-shilling fine. Even after the English Crown stepped in to repeal
the law, Massachusetts did not make Christmas a holiday until 1856, due to the
lingering sentiments and history.
- The [Omni Parker House][oph] converted its room 303 into a storage closet
due to the numerous associated paranormal events. People claimed to smell
whiskey and cigars, tied to a liquor salesman who reportedly died in that
room. Could this have inspired the King short story 1408?
- While not specific to Boston, the state bans bars from having "happy hour"
drinks. This followed a 1984 drunk-driving fatality, and Massachusetts does
[not permit bars to offer discounted drinks][nhh].
