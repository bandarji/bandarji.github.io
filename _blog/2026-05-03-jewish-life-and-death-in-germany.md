# History

Jews have experienced persecution throughout their history. The time spans,
politics and specific incidents number too many to cite. A people who
largely keep to themselves, trading amongst each other, maintaining their
own language, teaching in their own schools and having their own rituals, the
Jews generally ended up misunderstood and became convenient scapegoats. For
example, during the Black Death, many Jews stood trial for poisoning wells
and performing other acts to cause and continue the plague. In one incident,
around 2,000 Jews ended up burned alive in the *Valentine's Day Massacre*,
in Stasbourg, in 1349. Many Christians blamed the Jews for the killing of
Jesus, regardless of various churches opposing the thought. Persecutions
reached their most distructive under Nazi Germany policies, leading to The
Holocaust.

**Depicted Dislike of Jews By Christians**

![Christian Hate][christianhate]

By 1942, the Nazi leadership implemented the Final Solution, a way to
exterminate Jews entirely. This genocide transported Jews to death camps
where they awaited death by gassing or gunfire. Those not killed right away
experienced internment, where many died from malnutrition, starvation and
disease.

# Nazi Law Enforcement

The buildings that housed the Gestapo, Schutzstaffel Reich Security,
Sicherheitspolizei, Sicherheitsdienst and Einsatzgruppen, once stood on a
plot of land known as the Topography of Terror. These Nazi police and security
forces organized and carried out crimes against humanity.

**Topography of Terror**

People walk along what once was the basement of one of the security buildings.
A section of the Berlin Wall runs along the top (notice the dark round
coping). This section and the grounds closeby now stand as an indoor-outdoor
museum.

![Topography of Terror][tot]

# Responsibility of Germans

When the government repeatedly applies orders to a specific group of people,
what responsibility and culpability does the general population have? This
question haunts, divides and puzzles Germans to this day.

This board asks, "Did the German population under National Socialism have a
share of the responsibility for the Holocaust? Why?" Museum visitors write
their thoughts and place notes on the side of yes or no, or right on the
dividing line.

![Yes, No][responsible]

Born in England, Carey Harrison tattooed the first page of
*Minima Moralia - Reflections From Damaged Life* on his back. Even though
Berlin played a role in his family's persecution, Carey felt a strong
connection to his mother's native city. The book, written by a Germany author
during exile, contains essays reflecting on fascism and other -isms.

![Tattoo][tattoo]

# Monuments

Holocaust monuments exist in multiple places in Berlin. Many of them contain
uneven floors with large, non-uniform-height columns. Visitors experience
disorientation, to better understand the jarring effect of Jewish treatment
under Nazi rule, with deportations, forced labor and death camps.
