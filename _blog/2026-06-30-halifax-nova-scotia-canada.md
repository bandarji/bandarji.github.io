Nova Scotia translates from Latin as *New Scotland*. Briefly called
*Nova Francia* and *New Caledonia*, from French and Gaelic, Nova Scotia
started as a Canadian province on July 1, 1867, on the day Canada became a
country. The Province of Canada, which later became Ontario and Quebec, Nova
Scotia and New Brunswick joined on that day to form the *Dominion of Canada*.

Established in 1749, Halifax now houses 45% of Nova Scotia's residents. Like
Los Angeles, the city has a longer name, though no one uses it. Officially,
that's Halifax Regional Municipality, and kind of like LA, it refers to a
region in addition to an actual city.

Rather than hanging out at the boardwalk, near the ship terminal, we rented
a car to explore the countryside. Did not take many photos because it rained
most of the day.

*Three Churches*

![][p4]

We drove out to Mahone Bay, a town with an interesting feature. Along the
waterfront stand three churches, St James' Anglican, St John's
Evangelical Lutheran and Trinity United. In 1861, Presyterians built
Trinity United, no longer content to share Union Meeting House for worship
with Lutherans, Methodists and Baptists. By 1869, Lutherans had their own
church on the waterfront. Presbyterians moved their church to the waterfront
in 1885. The Anglican services started in their church by 1887. In 1925, the
Methodist and Presbyterian congregations, well, congregated, with the combined
churched named Trinity United. Today, the three make for a nice photo.

*George's Island*

![][p1]

Open to the public on weekends from June to November, George's Island once
served as a prison for Acadians, during the multi-year war to remove French
colonists from the area. These days, people take ferries to the island to
enjoy nature, have picnics and visit Fort Charlotte.

*Links To Norway*

![][p2]

The Norwegian memorial stone at Sackville Landing commemorates WWII sailors.
The face models after a similar stone in Larvik, Norway. The back names 30
Norwegian men buried in Nova Scotian cemetaries.

> \
> In memory of members of\

> Royal Norwegian Navy\
