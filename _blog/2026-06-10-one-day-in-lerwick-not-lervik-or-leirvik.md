# Lerwick

Shetland's [Lerwick][lw] means "Bay of Clay", derived from the Old Norse
words "leir" (clay) and "vik" (bay). Norway has the [Leirvik][lv1] and
[Lervik][lv2] towns, and Faroe Islands has [Leirvik][lv3]. Confused yet?

The main town of Shetland, with a population of under 7,000, Lerwick remains
the northernmost major settlement of the United Kingdom.

# Clickimin Broch

![][i0]

Excavated of [Clickimin Broch][cb] started in 1953. No one knows its original
purpose, with theories wildly varying from a place of worship to a site for
coronations to a fancy home or fort.

# Lodberries

In the early 18th century, the people of Lerwick started constructing
warehouses so that boats could dock within the building, to ease goods'
unloading. These merchant houses also provided cover for smugglers to bring
in contraband, such as tobacco and alcohol.

**A Lodberry**

![][i3]

Built right into the bay, this stone warehouse is known as a lodberry.

**Renamed**

![][i4]

Everyone take note. Copland's Pier *used* to sit on the side of Copland's
Lodberry. Now, we refer to that as Gillie's Pier.

# The Knab

![][i2]

This area of the shore is known as *The Knab*. Historically, customs
officials would watch boats coming to port from this point. These days, locals
know The Knab as the hangout for courting couples.
